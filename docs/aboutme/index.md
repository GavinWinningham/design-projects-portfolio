1. My name is Gavin Winnichem. I am a junior in mechanical engineering at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte. I was initially drawn to mechanical engineering with all the time I spent working on my car and learning alongside my friends. Originally, what started as an interest in trying to understand cars and modifying turned into a much deeper interest in how complex systems work, how things can be improved, and how to make them more efficient. Since then, my curiosity has continued to grow into many aspects of my life, including building computers, building robots, and paying much more attention to the deeper role engineering plays in infrastructure.
As an engineer I find that I am someone who is motivated by my curiosity and interest in problem solving, specifically interested in automotive engineering, with a strong interest in aerodynamics and wind tunnel testing. Being able to see how design choices directly affect things in practice and watch something become more efficient and productive from changes is something I deeply find myself interested in. I also find interest in energy and infrastructure, specifically with the transmission and distribution of energy. As AI grows and data centers are created, the demand for energy will only increase larger, and I'm interested to see where this will take us in the future and learn as the world starts to change.
Several experiences have shaped the type of engineer I am becoming. My training at Sandvik Coromant gave me a better understanding of subtractive manufacturing and production as a whole. It showed me how engineering problem solving is utilized in the real world, and specifically in production settings. My involvement in Omicron Delta Kappa introduced me to very high level thinking and motivated students, pushing me further towards my goals. Serving as a teacher's assistant in the mechanical engineering department has also been a significant part of my journey. It has strengthened my ability to explain complex ideas clearly to others and to be able to communicate efficiently and effectively. In conclusion these experiences have come together to create the engineer I am today. I hope to continue to grow further into a professional engineer who can think critically, communicate clearly, and contribute to the challenges ahead in my career.

3. What it means to me to defend an engineering decision is to fully justify your process and thinking for the decisions that you've made. I believe that if you are defending an engineering decision, you stand by it. And if that means putting your job on the line, that's the case. Whether it's at the cost of resources, you stand by it through the logic, criteria, and the trade offs that were considered. And you understand why and how that final choice you have stumbled upon fits the problem better than others. As for now, I believe I could defend an engineering decision, though I still have a lot to learn and improve upon. I believe that my overall understanding and decision making requires some type of responsibility and or reasoning. So as I become a better engineer, I hope to learn how to make precise justifications with more structured decision making and ideas so I can fully defend an engineering decision.
